How to Play

The table is a pyramid, unfolded

Four players hold four courts: South ♠ (you), East ♥, North ♣ and West ♦. Each court is one triangular face of a pyramid. Its three subjects (Ace to 10) are the face's three corners: left and right on the ground, centre at the top. Its three royals (Jack, Queen, King) sit beneath them and rule them.

Neighbouring faces share a corner on the ground; all four share the top. When the cards at a shared corner match, those two corners meet. When every shared corner meets, the faces close into a pyramid.

Lanes

A lane is a pivot between two courts. There are ten:

  • Four base corners: your right subject and East's left subject, and so on round the table.
  • Four ridges: your centre subject and each neighbour's centre subject.
  • Two peaks: your centre and North's centre, and East's centre and West's. These are the cross beneath the apex. You can swap across them, but they make no gems.

A lane is paired when its two subjects match in rank. Suits don't matter. Aces are wild: an ace pairs with anything, and two aces make one.

Control

Each lane is ruled by the royals under its two subjects (left under left, centre under centre, right under right). Whoever's royal trumps controls the lane.

  • Ranks: King beats Queen, Queen beats Jack, Jack beats King.
  • Equal ranks go to the suit: ♠ beats ♥, ♥ beats ♣, ♣ beats ♦, ♦ beats ♠.
  • Same rank and same colour is a stalemate: nobody controls that lane.

Your turn

Every action costs one gem. Each turn begins with one free action.

  • Swap two of your subjects: tap one, then the other. No lane needed.
  • Swap two of your royals to change who controls your lanes.
  • Swap across a lane you control: tap one card, then its partner across the lane.
  • Swap across someone else's lane by paying its controller: your gem goes to them.
  • Draw: tap the deck when it glows (hand of up to 3), then play a hand card by tapping it and then one of your subjects. The replaced card goes to the bottom of the deck.
  • End your turn when you're done.

Gems

Gems never form during a turn. They form in a generation phase: at the start of the game, and between every two turns. In each phase, every paired base corner and ridge forms a gem, and so does the apex when all four centre cards match, and every court holding a flush. A node holds at most one gem.

Whoever controls a node collects its gem automatically, at once (the apex goes to whoever's centre royal beats all three others). A gem on a stalemated node waits until someone controls it, even mid-turn.

The end

When all ten lanes are paired the pyramid is complete and the game ends. Whoever makes that final pair takes the capstone: +5 gems. Then most gems wins. If the round clock runs out first, the game ends there, with no capstone.
